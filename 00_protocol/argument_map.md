# Argument Map

## Scientific tension

A simple, clinically interpretable set of current-health variables adds substantial predictive ranking information in every unseen national cohort. The same models can still assign materially wrong absolute probabilities, so the very evidence that supports risk stratification creates a sharper question: when can those probabilities guide action?

## Central question

Does current multidomain health improve prediction beyond age and sex in completely held-out national cohorts, do those ranking gains imply accurate absolute risk, and when does local intercept recalibration add decision value?

## Evidence chain

1. Exact common outcome and complete-cohort holdout establish a credible cross-national transport test.
2. M1 improves AUC in all 4 holdouts: the clean replicated positive finding.
3. CHARLS and MHAS show large opposing probability errors, including worse overall accuracy in MHAS: the positive-to-tension reversal.
4. Pooled random splits conceal at least 1 target failure in 99/100 repetitions: the general validation innovation.
5. Equal-cohort DCA yields substantial gains chiefly in CHARLS and MHAS: the practical increment.
6. Robustness analyses define success and failure boundaries; the HRS person-equal test corroborates internal direction only and remains exploratory.

## Central thesis

Current multidomain health transports useful risk ranking across national cohorts, but probability-based use requires complete target holdout because pooled validation can hide clinically relevant absolute-risk errors. Selective, independently evaluated intercept updating adds value chiefly where those target errors are substantial.

## Counterarguments and limits

Ranking/calibration separation is conceptually known; this study's increment is the 4-cohort empirical combination of replicated incremental discrimination, direct pooled-concealment testing, and disjoint updating evaluation. Country is inseparable from survey, language, era, follow-up, and measurement. Four targets cannot establish a universal trigger. The retrospective study does not test implementation impact.

## Final move

End with an affirmative evaluation pathway: complete target holdout, probability-scale assessment, selective updating, and independent post-update evaluation.
